# Pitch template

Fill only with what survived the roast. Every claim traces to something the user actually said or something recon established. If a section can't be filled honestly, write the gap in one line instead of padding it. A visible gap is credible; filler is not.

Target length: one page. Nine sections, a few lines each. Cut anything that isn't load-bearing.

---

## `<Name>`

**One-liner.** For `<specific who>`, `<what it does>`, instead of `<what they do today>`. No adjectives, no "AI-powered", no "revolutionary".

**The problem.** Who hurts, how often, and what it costs them. Concrete and quantified where possible. One anecdote beats one adjective.

**Today's alternative.** What they use now, including "nothing" and "a spreadsheet", and the specific point where it breaks.

**The insight.** What you know that isn't obvious. If this section is empty the pitch has no spine, so go back to the roast.

**What it does.** The mechanism, plainly. A reader should be able to picture the first screen or the first command. No architecture diagram.

**Why now.** The specific change that makes this possible or necessary today and wouldn't have two years ago.

**The wedge.** The narrow first slice you win outright, and what it opens up next. Name the slice, don't describe the whole market.

**Business model.** Who pays, how much, how often. Unit cost to serve one user. The arithmetic, not the ambition. Omit this section for a side project.

**Traction and evidence.** Anything real: conversations held, waitlist, a working prototype, a paying user, a competitor's pricing page proving the category exists. If there's none, say "none yet" and go straight to the next section.

**Risks and how we find out.** Two or three, worst first, each with the test that resolves it and a rough timebox. This is the section that makes an experienced audience trust the rest of the page.

**The ask.** What you want from the person reading: money, a pilot customer, an introduction, a co-founder, two hours of their time. Be specific about the amount and what it buys.

## Delivery notes

Add a short block at the end of the file, outside the pitch, holding the three questions this audience will ask first (drawn from the unresolved items in `roast.md`) and the honest one-sentence answer to each. Not part of the pitch. It's the prep.
