# Attack lines and named failure modes

A bank to draw from, not a checklist to run. Pick the three or four that bite hardest on *this* idea. An irrelevant question spends the user's patience and teaches nothing.

## Named failure modes

Check the idea against these by name. If one fits, say which and why.

- **Vitamin, not painkiller.** Nice to have. Nobody changes behavior for nice.
- **Solution in search of a problem.** The idea started from a technology or a clever mechanism, and the user was reverse-engineered to fit it.
- **Founder is the only user.** The pain is real, but sample size one. Common and survivable if admitted, fatal if unnoticed.
- **Feature, not a company.** The incumbent ships it in a sprint and it becomes a checkbox on their pricing page.
- **Free alternative is good enough.** A spreadsheet, a Slack channel, a cron job, or doing nothing. "Do nothing" is the strongest competitor in most markets.
- **Two-sided cold start.** Needs supply and demand simultaneously, and neither side shows up first.
- **Sale requires a committee.** The user loves it, but three other people have to say yes and one is legal.
- **No wedge.** The plan is to be better overall, everywhere, at once, rather than undeniably better at one narrow thing.
- **Distribution unsolved.** The product is fine, but there is no answer to how the first hundred users hear about it that isn't "post on Twitter".
- **Economics inverted.** Cost per user (infra, inference, support, hardware, ops) grows at least as fast as revenue per user.
- **Model-shaped, not product-shaped.** A thin wrapper whose entire value gets absorbed by the next platform release.
- **Regulatory wall.** The fastest version is the illegal one, and the legal version is a different, slower business.
- **Trust barrier.** Technically works, but nobody hands a stranger's tool the access, the data, or the liability it needs.
- **Wrong time horizon.** Correct thesis, five years early, and the runway is eighteen months.

## Round 1: the indifferent user

- Walk me through the last time you personally hit this problem. What did you actually do?
- Who is the first user? Name a person or a company, not a persona.
- What are they using today, and what specifically is bad about it? "It's clunky" isn't an answer.
- What do they have to stop doing to start using this? Who notices that change?
- Has anyone outside your head asked for this? What exactly did they say?
- Would they pay, or would they just say they'd pay? Has anyone paid?
- If it disappeared after a month, would they be annoyed or relieved?

## Round 2: the investor

- Why doesn't this exist yet? Someone smart has had this idea; what stopped them, and is that thing gone?
- Why now? Name the specific change: a cost curve, a regulation, a platform, a behavior shift.
- What's the wedge, the narrowest slice where you are undeniably the best option?
- Where's the money? Who signs, how much, how often, and what's the unit cost of serving them?
- What's the cost to acquire one paying user, and how did you get that number?
- What does the incumbent do when this works? What's your answer to it?
- What gets better as you grow, whether data, network, distribution or cost, or are you just working harder?
- Why you? What do you know or have that a competent stranger doesn't?
- What's the smallest outcome that would still be worth the next two years of your life?

## Round 3: the builder

- What's the hardest single piece to build, and is there a version of this that doesn't need it?
- What breaks first at 10x usage, and is that a real problem or a nice one?
- Which assumption, if false, kills the whole thing? Not the top three. The one.
- What is the cheapest thing you could do this week that would tell you whether that assumption holds?
- What are you *not* building, on purpose, in v1?
- How does it fail in front of a user, and what happens then?
- What's the ongoing operational burden (moderation, support, data upkeep, on-call) and who does it at 3am?
- If it half-works in 18 months, is that a business or a job you can't quit?

## Reading answers

- **Concrete beats confident.** A named customer, a real number with a source, a screenshot of a complaint: those close holes. Conviction does not.
- **Watch for scope inflation under pressure.** If a challenge is answered by making the idea bigger ("we'll also do X"), that's a dodge. Push back toward the wedge.
- **"Nobody has built this" cuts both ways.** Sometimes it means the market is unproven, not open. Establish which.
- **Distinguish "I don't know" from a hand-wave.** "I don't know, here's how I'd find out" is a good answer and should be credited as one. A confident non-answer is worse than an admission.
